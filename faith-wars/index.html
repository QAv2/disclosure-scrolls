Act II

The Severance

For the first three centuries after the death of Jesus of Nazareth, there was no single Christian church. There were dozens of communities across the Mediterranean, each with different texts, different practices, different understandings of what the teacher had meant. Some believed in a hierarchy of priests. Others believed every person had direct access to the divine — that the kingdom of heaven was already inside you, and no intermediary was required. The Greek word for this direct knowing was gnosis. The people who practiced it were called Gnostics.

They were about to be erased.

Documented
In 325 AD, Emperor Constantine I — unbaptized, fresh from unifying the Roman Empire — convened the First Council of Nicaea to resolve the Arian controversy, Easter dating, and 20 disciplinary canons. Attendance was traditionally numbered at 318 bishops (a symbolic count; contemporary eyewitness Eusebius of Caesarea estimated “more than 250”). The council produced the Nicene Creed, binding imperial power to orthodox doctrine. Only 2 bishops — Theonas of Marmarica and Secundus of Ptolemais — refused to sign and were exiled alongside the Alexandrian presbyter Arius of Alexandria. Constantine ordered Arius’s writings burned; anyone caught hiding them faced the death penalty (Socrates Scholasticus I.9). Nicaea did not vote on the biblical canon; over the following decades (Athanasius’s 39th Festal Letter of 367 AD and the regional Councils of Hippo in 393 and Carthage in 397), church authorities formalized the 27-book New Testament while 50+ known early Christian texts remained outside it, evaluated by apostolicity, antiquity, widespread liturgical use, and conformity with orthodox doctrine (the regula fidei).

Credible
In December 1945, an Egyptian farmer named Muhammad Ali al-Samman discovered 12 leather-bound codices plus 8 leaves of a 13th codex buried in a sealed jar near Nag Hammadi, Egypt. Scrap papyrus (cartonnage) inside the bindings carries dated receipts up to 348 AD, establishing that the books were bound no earlier than the mid-fourth century. Scholar James M. Robinson hypothesized they were hidden around 367 AD — the year Bishop Athanasius of Alexandria issued his 39th Festal Letter listing the 27-book New Testament canon and condemning heretical apocrypha as inventions to be rejected (while still allowing a middle tier of non-canonical catechetical readings); newer scholarship (Hugo Lundhaug and Lance Jenott, 2015) argues they circulated inside mainstream Pachomian monasteries and were buried in the late fourth or fifth century. The Nag Hammadi library contained 52 tractates (~45–46 distinct works), including Gnostic gospels describing a radically different Christianity: one where salvation came through direct inner knowledge (gnosis), not solely through institutional mediation (pistis). The texts lay hidden for roughly 1,500–1,600 years.

The scrolls revealed that Judaism before Christianity was far more diverse than any institution had admitted. Full publication took 62 years.

Act III

The Burning

The canon was decided. The institution was built. But the impulse toward direct experience didn’t stop. Across the next sixteen centuries, dissident movements, mystics, indigenous traditions, and wandering teachers kept emerging who rejected institutional permission to access the divine. Again and again, movements that bypassed authorized mediation were not debated — they were crushed.

And if you track the method of destruction across cultures and centuries, a pattern emerges that has nothing to do with theology and everything to do with access control.

Credible
The Cathars (known locally as “Good Men” and “Good Women”) were a dissident Christian movement in southern France that rejected Roman Catholic priestly authority, church wealth, and sacraments. Traditional historians (such as Malcolm Barber) document a two-tier dualist community of ascetic perfecti (parfaits) and believers bound by the laying-on-of-hands consolamentum rite, while revisionist scholars (Mark Gregory Pegg, R.I. Moore) argue the Church projected an organized “Cathar” counter-church onto local dissident communities. Either way, in 1209, Pope Innocent III launched the Albigensian Crusade — a 20-year military campaign to eradicate them. At the sack of Béziers (July 22, 1209), Papal legate Arnaud Amalric reported “nearly 20,000” killed in his letter to Innocent III (modern historians estimate the town’s total population at 10,000–14,000, the vast majority orthodox Catholics). German Cistercian chronicler Caesarius of Heisterbach (c. 1220) later attributed the infamous command to Amalric: “Kill them all. God will know his own.” At Montségur in 1244, over 200 Cathars walked into a bonfire rather than recant. The last known Cathar parfait, Guillaume Bélibaste, was burned in 1321.

Documented
Giordano Bruno was a Dominican friar who proposed that the universe was infinite, that stars were distant suns with their own planets, and that direct experience of the cosmos was communion with the divine. After an 8-year trial by the Roman Inquisition — condemned primarily for theological heresies (denying the Trinity, Christ’s divinity, Mary’s virginity, and transubstantiation) alongside his infinite-universe cosmology — he was burned alive on February 17, 1600, in the Campo de’ Fiori in Rome. His tongue was clamped with a wooden-and-iron gag (mordacchia) so he could not address the crowd at the pyre. His works remained on the Index of Forbidden Books for 366 years (1600–1966). In the exact square where he was burned, a statue of him stands — erected in 1889 over Vatican objections.

#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""
Extract files hidden in a CNTYSEED steganographic image.
Only requires: pip install Pillow
Usage: python3 extract.py <image.png> <output_dir>
"""
import hashlib, io, os, struct, sys, tarfile, zlib
from PIL import Image

MAGIC = b"CNTYSEED"
HDR_SIZE = 64
HDR_FMT = "!8sBBBBII32sHH8s"


def lsb_decode(flat, depth, num_bytes):
    total_bits = num_bytes * 8
    pad = depth - (total_bits % depth) if total_bits % depth else 0
    slots = (total_bits + pad) // depth
    mask = (1 << depth) - 1
    bits = []
    for i in range(slots):
        val = flat[i] & mask
        for shift in range(depth - 1, -1, -1):
            bits.append((val >> shift) & 1)
    out = bytearray()
    for i in range(0, num_bytes * 8, 8):
        byte = 0
        for b in range(8):
            byte = (byte << 1) | bits[i + b]
        out.append(byte)
    return bytes(out)


def extract(image_path, output_dir):
    img = Image.open(image_path).convert("RGB")
    flat = [ch for px in img.getdata() for ch in px]

    hdr = None
    for depth in (1, 2, 3, 4):
        raw = lsb_decode(flat, depth, HDR_SIZE)
        if raw[:8] != MAGIC:
            continue
        fields = struct.unpack(HDR_FMT, raw)
        if fields[3] == depth:
            hdr = {
                "flags": fields[2], "lsb_depth": depth,
                "compression": fields[4], "original_size": fields[5],
                "compressed_size": fields[6], "sha256": fields[7],
            }
            break

    if not hdr:
        print("No embedded data found.")
        sys.exit(1)

    if hdr["flags"] & 0x01:
        print("This image is encrypted.")
        sys.exit(1)

    print(f"Found: {hdr['original_size']:,} bytes, {hdr['lsb_depth']}-LSB")

    total = HDR_SIZE + hdr["compressed_size"]
    raw = lsb_decode(flat, hdr["lsb_depth"], total)
    payload = raw[HDR_SIZE:]

    if hdr["compression"] == 1:
        payload = zlib.decompress(payload)
    elif hdr["compression"] == 2:
        import lzma
        payload = lzma.decompress(payload)

    if hashlib.sha256(payload).digest() == hdr["sha256"]:
        print("Integrity: VERIFIED")
    else:
        print("WARNING: SHA-256 mismatch")

    os.makedirs(output_dir, exist_ok=True)
    if hdr["flags"] & 0x02:
        with tarfile.open(fileobj=io.BytesIO(payload), mode="r") as tar:
            tar.extractall(path=output_dir, filter="data")
        print(f"Extracted to: {output_dir}/")
    else:
        with open(os.path.join(output_dir, "extracted"), "wb") as f:
            f.write(payload)
        print(f"Written to: {output_dir}/extracted")


if __name__ == "__main__":
    if len(sys.argv) != 3:
        print(f"Usage: {sys.argv[0]} <image.png> <output_dir>")
        sys.exit(1)
    extract(sys.argv[1], sys.argv[2])
